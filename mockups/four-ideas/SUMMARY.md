# Four website ideas for Muhammad Haris

Four genuinely different directions, all light. Each PNG is one full homepage, top to bottom, at 1440px wide; the HTML is in `src/`. Each idea has its own layout, typeface, colour, components and voice, so no two read alike.

## What the references taught (measured, not guessed)

**schemasauce.com.** It is copy-led: a modest 40px Manrope headline, 19px body text at 1.7 line height, square 5px buttons and very few images (a portrait and one event photo). The voice is blunt ("No side quests. No BS."). It doubles as a knowledge hub (tech topics, user groups, newsletter), and the header does not stick or animate.

**galenpyle.com.** It is proof and image-led: a wide-tracked 55px caps headline, many generated images, offers split by company size, a "Trusted By" logo wall, and a Calendly booking calendar on the page itself. The header does not stick, and nothing animates on scroll.

What carried into the ideas below:
- Schema's plain-spoken voice: Spec Sheet.
- Schema's knowledge hub: the five architecture write-ups on GitHub appear in Spec Sheet, The Review and Canvas.
- Galen's segmentation: Canvas, with its inputs and outputs map.
- Galen's on-page booking: Hello's in-chat time picker and Canvas's closing frame.

All copy uses Haris's real material: his title, how he works, project figures checked against each project, the Upwork record and real client quotes. Grey boxes are where new photos and clips go.

## 01-spec-sheet.png: Spec Sheet
**Skills:** industrial-brutalist-ui (Swiss industrial print), design-taste-frontend.
**Look:** matte paper, carbon-black text, one hazard red, a visible grid and square corners. Heavy uppercase Archivo carries the structure, and IBM Plex Mono is used for every label and figure.
**Voice:** terse and exact ("Production AI. Specified, built, shipped.").
**Sections:**
- the four numbers as a data strip;
- capabilities as a spec table with reference codes;
- projects as "deployment records" with fields and a live status;
- warning-stripe divider and four operating principles;
- "field reports" for the reviews;
- a request form laid out like a technical form.

**Best for:** a serious, engineering-first impression.

## 02-the-review.png: The Review
**Skills:** minimalist-ui editorial, the brand skill's voice framework.
**Look:** the homepage is a small magazine about his work. White paper, Libre Caslon serif, small Franklin labels and an ultramarine accent.
**Voice:** warm and narrative; it tells stories instead of listing services.
**Sections:**
- a masthead and a cover story ("The engineer who stays until it ships");
- an "In this issue" contents strip;
- a three-column feature on the broadband support project, with a drop cap, a pull quote and verified figures;
- "Five questions for Haris" as an interview;
- client reviews as "Letters";
- a booking box styled like a subscription card, and a colophon footer.

**Best for:** standing out as a thoughtful senior independent.

## 03-hello.png: Hello
**Skills:** ui-ux-pro-max (AI-native, conversational pattern), emil-design-eng polish, Galen's on-page booking.
**Look:** bright white, soft pastel tiles, rounded Gabarito headings and a coral accent.
**Voice:** casual and friendly ("Tell me what's slowing your team down").
**Sections:**
- a hero built as a chat, showing what an agent does with real figures (6,700+ conversations, 7 departments), with a message box to describe your problem;
- "Things I can take off your plate" as pastel tiles;
- the numbers with sticker badges and recent work;
- "How we'd work together" written as a short chat thread;
- reviews as colourful speech bubbles;
- a booking card where you pick a time in the chat.

**Best for:** approachability and the most conversions from non-technical owners.

## 04-canvas.png: Canvas
**Skills:** gpt-taste, stitch-design-taste.
**Look:** the whole site is an open design file. A dot-grid canvas, named frames, Instrument Sans, handwritten Caveat notes and an electric-blue accent.
**Voice:** clear and systematic ("I map the work. Then I build the system.").
**Sections:**
- a floating toolbar as the navigation;
- a hero map: support queue, inbox and quotes flow into an AI agent, which flows out to the CRM, the customer and a person taking over;
- the four services as "components", one shown selected;
- each project drawn as a small flow with its result;
- the process as a kanban board;
- reviews as sticky notes next to an Upwork record card;
- a closing frame with a comment bubble from Haris.

**Best for:** showing exactly how he thinks and builds.

Checks run on all four:
- ui-ux-pro-max contrast: every text colour passes 4.5:1 (large text 3:1).
- One call-to-action label per page.
- No em dashes.
- No invented figures.

Nothing on the live site has been changed. Pick one (or mix parts), and the build starts from there.
