One agent. Six workflows. Seven departments.

For a UK fibre broadband provider, the agent talks to customers and six single-purpose workflows do the work.

A UK fibre broadband provider wanted its customers answered at any hour. Every enquiry needed someone to read it, look up the account, check the network and route it to the right team.

Haris built a chat agent that drives the conversation and calls six stateless backend workflows as internal APIs. Each one does a single job: looking up the account, checking for an outage, routing a ticket to one of seven departments, emailing the customer.

The conversation layer stays replaceable. The backend stays testable.

Keeping the workflows stateless and single-purpose is what later let a second channel, a phone voice agent, reuse the same backend without a rewrite.

The system also reports on itself. A live dashboard, built as part of the same engagement, tracks conversation volume, the split between existing and new customers, and how often the AI needed a human, by department and by whether it happened inside opening hours.

What does a forward deployed engineer actually do?
I work inside a client's team, find the work that should be running on AI, and build it through to production.
When do you tell a client not to build something?
When the honest answer is "don't". I would rather say so before either of us spends money finding out.
How do you keep a system safe to change?
Everything ships in small reviewed pull requests, with tests and CI on every change.
No-code tools or real code?
Both. n8n, Make and Power Automate where they are the right tool, and Next.js, Python and FastAPI when they are not.
